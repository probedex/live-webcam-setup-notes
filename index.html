In Character

A host guide for running a tabletop game night on Discord where players can appear on camera as the characters they play, using a browser live face swap.

Status, October 2026: the live studio at LiveFaceSwap.io is not connected yet. The camera preview is free and needs no login. Use this guide to prepare your table, then run the live part once sessions open.

The idea

Role-play groups already draw portraits, write backstories and do voices. Cameras usually break the spell: you hear a dwarf and see someone's kitchen. LiveFaceSwap.io describes Discord D&D sessions as one of its use cases: players join through OBS Virtual Camera using portraits they drew or have rights to, so the picture on camera matches the character at the table.

Because the studio redraws the webcam picture as it happens, expressions carry through. When the rogue grins, the portrait grins.

Session zero: agree on the rules

  • Everyone opts in. Cameras in character are optional. Nobody is pressured to use one.
  • Adults only. The service is for people 18 and older. Each session starts with four confirmations: 18 or older, rights to every face used, and two misuse rules from the Terms.
  • Own art or licensed art. Use a portrait you drew, one you commissioned with permission to use it, or an original character from the built-in library. The library's 46 images are originals with no real people.
  • No real faces of people who aren't at the table. The point is the character, not someone else.
  • Say it out loud. The service asks users to let others on a call know about the swap. At a game table that is easy: "Tonight I'm on camera as my character."

Picking a look for your character

  1. Face swap with your own portrait upload (PNG, JPEG or WebP, used once it passes a content check), or one of the 30 library characters.
  2. Try on one of the 10 library outfits if you want to keep your face but change your costume.
  3. Restyle the whole frame. For a one-shot, the game master might choose ink comic for a noir mystery, watercolor for a fairy-tale campaign, pixel art for a retro dungeon crawl or neon cyber for a sci-fi heist. Anime and clay are the other two looks.

Setup for each player

  1. Open the studio in Chrome on a Windows or Mac computer and allow your webcam. No GPU or install is needed for the studio; frames are redrawn in the cloud.
  2. Pick your reference and open the clean output window. It is 960×540 and shows only the AI picture.
  3. Install the free OBS Studio. Add the output window as a Window Capture source and fit it to the canvas.
  4. Click Start Virtual Camera in OBS.
  5. In Discord, open User Settings > Voice & Video and choose OBS Virtual Camera.
  6. Join the voice channel and turn your camera on. Keep the studio output window open the whole night.

Game master tips

  • Switch faces between scenes, not mid-sentence. Changing the reference between takes is how the site suggests playing several characters; for a game master, that means one portrait per important NPC, swapped at scene breaks.
  • Plan session time. One live session runs per account at a time, and Live X2.0 uses 1 credit per second. A ten-minute in-character scene is 600 credits. Consider saving the swap for key moments, such as introductions, a villain's reveal or a dramatic monologue, and spending the rest of the night on regular cameras.
  • Have a fallback. If the studio reports a camera that is busy, access denied or blocked, no camera, or no picture for 60 seconds, switch Discord back to your regular webcam and keep playing. A dropped connection reconnects once on its own.

Recording the highlight

The studio's built-in recorder downloads WebM or MP4 depending on your browser, with an AI generated mark in the lower-right corner. Recordings stay on your device; camera frames are not saved by the service. Ask the table before sharing any clip with people outside it.

What it costs a group

Since one live session runs per account at a time, each player who appears in character uses their own account. Opening the camera is free. New accounts get 180 credits (valid 30 days, about 3 minutes), enough to test the setup during session zero. Plans start at $15/month; credit packs are one-time and never expire, which suits groups that only meet now and then.
